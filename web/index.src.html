The tempo

On the OP-XY, Brain Dance is a sample engine, as Recur and Grains are, because sample engines receive the project tempo every block. With no sample it plays its own synthesis; give it one and each lane plays the best snippet it finds there. All its voices share one clock in 32nd notes. With the sequencer playing it holds to the song position within a block; stopped, the first key restarts it. Press Sequencer play and Click to hear it lock.

The algorithms encoder 1

lanes Autechre-style polymeter: euclidean lanes of 16, 12, 10 and 7 steps whose rotations drift with chaos.
break Aphex-style: a two-bar amen read a 16th at a time; chaos chops and stutters it, glitch rolls the snare upward.
data Ikeda-style: a bar of 32nds from a register, repeated exactly; sine pulses and bit noise under hard gates.
euclid A clean skeleton; later keys play 12 and 10 against 16.
pulse Pacemaker neurons on the 16th grid, one a lane, each nudging the next round a ring: interlocking figures that repeat.
shred A bass sweeping down on every 16th in sixes against eights, gated noise in its rests, clicks in triplet stutters. Try 180 BPM.
1 to 9 Each algorithm has nine more variants on the same encoder (shrd1 to shrd9): other cycle lengths and patterns, other lanes leading, ratchets in thirds or fifths.

What you're hearing

The audio is the machine's own engine, sample.cpp, compiled to WebAssembly and run as the OP-XY would run it: four lanes a key (low, snap, click, blip), all tuned to the key, in 64-sample blocks at 44.1 kHz. A release stands in for the OP-XY's amp envelope; its filter, LFOs and effects aren't here.
